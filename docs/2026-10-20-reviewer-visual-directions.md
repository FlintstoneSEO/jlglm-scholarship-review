# Reviewer visual directions: decision previews

## Brief and boundaries

User requests three image mockups informed by Mobbin before selecting a more professional reviewer design. Audience: Business Growth Grant reviewers reading application evidence, checking documents and prerequisites, scoring independently, saving drafts and certifying submission. Mode: Operate. Scope: image concepts only; no application implementation or new IA is approved by this exploration.

Current evidence: October 20 dashboard/queue source, shared ReviewWorkspace and Grant detail source, Phase E tokens, seven-criterion local practice fixture and existing rendered practice screenshot. Current authenticated browser capture remains unavailable. Screenshot is historical baseline, not a current render. Existing material is sufficient to explore composition, but not to certify live usability.

All previews depict the same fictional Capital City Repair application and clearly label sample data. Seven existing fixture criteria total 100 points: Business Narrative & Value Proposition 15; Financial Performance & Business Health 15; Growth Opportunity 15; Use of Grant Funds 20; Expected Business Impact & Measurable Outcomes 15; Business Capacity & Financial Management 10; Why This Grant, and Why Now? 10. Sample scores 12, 11, 13 and four unscored yield 36/100, 3 of 7 scored. This is an incomplete draft, never a funding recommendation. Certification remains required and Submit Review disabled. No peer scores, rankings, automatic decisions, invented integrations or autosave.

Preserve Justice League black #050505, green #006633, yellow #f4b400, white, neutral #f7f7f5, text #171a18, muted #59615c and border #d9dedb. Inter and Lucide remain. Exploration permits proposed shell rearrangements for comparison; selection is needed before any implementation. No new fonts, dark reading mode, glass, photography or ornamental graphs.

## A. Committee Desk

Family: Institutional Clarity. Strategy: make all seven scoring criteria and their completion states immediately scannable. Emotion: precise, dependable. Improve existing black rail to a compact, clearly grouped navigation column; keep white canvas and restrained green selection. Main record title anchors a wide rubric ledger, with compact independent review summary to the right. Dense rows, generous separation between identity, evaluation and actions. Numeric columns align; criterion titles use normal case and readable weights. Yellow belongs only to draft state and active navigation. No large dashboard metrics or nested card grid. Thin dividers and modest 6-8px radius; primary actions use green, secondary Save draft uses outline. No decorative motion.

Mobile: rail becomes existing accessible navigation disclosure; rubric rows become stacked labeled criterion/score pairs; summary follows the criteria. Preserve all controls and explicit save. Fit: high for experienced repeat reviewers. Risk: whole-rubric density and supporting-document switching. Accessibility: label score inputs, preserve 44px targets and logical source order; test long criterion names. Performance: normal shared React primitives; no new asset/library needs. Distinct from B/C: rubric overview dominates, existing full rail remains, all criteria visible simultaneously. Status: strong low-migration alternative.

## B. Evidence & Evaluation

Family: Editorial Evidence adapted to operational sans typography. Strategy: place evidence and scoring in one reading workspace. Emotion: considered, composed, authoritative. Restructure shell into a compact black masthead with labeled navigation and clear program context. Below record identity and existing Overview/Application/Documents/Rubric navigation, use broad reading pane and narrower rubric pane. Source text dominates the left; aligned scoring fields and review state occupy the right. Sparse internal divisions, open white reading area, subtle neutral canvas. Keep same Inter hierarchy; larger business identity and body text, modest section headings. Green for selected evaluation/actions only. No serif editorial costume, no imagery and no colored card edges.

Mobile: switch explicitly between Application and Rubric using existing section concepts, preserving mounted input/state and document position where practical; summary/save remains reachable without overlaying content. Fit: highest for evidence-heavy grant evaluation. Risk: split view requires enough width and careful persistence/keyboard testing; source-to-criterion mapping must never be inferred as a scoring rule. Accessibility: two labeled regions, predictable focus, stacked layout below viable split width. Performance: reuse loaded content and shared rubric; lazy-load real document viewer as current behavior permits. Distinct: horizontal shell, broad narrative reading surface, side-by-side assessment, calmer rhythm. Status: recommended for user choice, not implementation authorization.

## C. Focused Review

Family: precision workspace/master-detail. Strategy: retain queue context while concentrating on one criterion at a time. Emotion: controlled, efficient, reassuring. Replace broad rail in the concept with narrow icon rail carrying accessible labels/tooltips and an adjacent application list. Main region emphasizes one current criterion and its supporting excerpt; a seven-item criterion navigator shows scored/unscored states. Substantial whitespace is reserved for reading and notes rather than decorative empty space. Stable bottom action strip distinguishes draft save from gated submission. Review progress is compact text, never an ornamental progress ring. Same Justice League tokens and Inter, stronger task hierarchy than A, less simultaneous scoring than B.

Mobile: show queue as its own screen; selected application opens a focused criterion with labeled previous/next navigation and full rubric access. Do not force linear completion or bypass prerequisite gates. Fit: strong for concentration and occasional reviewers. Risk: hidden criteria and extra navigation, plus broader workspace state work than A. Accessibility: no icon-only unlabeled controls, focus restoration on queue/detail changes, numeric score controls remain native. Performance: existing data; no peer read boundaries. Distinct: narrow rail plus collection pane, single-criterion focus, continuous queue context and stepped task rhythm. Status: alternate requiring user selection and documented state/navigation spec before implementation.

## Decision

User selected B and authorized implementation across all Business Growth Grant sections on October 20, 2026. The supplied concept image and written specification are the approved direction. See concept-b-implementation.md for the implementation scope and preservation boundaries. Generated images are illustrative concept previews, not functional screenshots or accessibility proof. Future implementation must use existing shared mechanics with program-specific content, preserve all authorization/scoring rules, and pass rendered responsive and workflow validation.
